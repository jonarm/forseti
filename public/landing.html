The problem

Most organisations cannot name every AI system they run.

Let alone say who owns it, what it may be given, or whether a person checks its output. That is the question a customer's security review now opens with — and the one an ISO 42001 auditor closes with.

3×

Shadow AI outruns procurement

Tools arrive on a company card and an SSO login. By the time governance hears about one, it is already in a customer-facing workflow.

Weeks

Every questionnaire starts from zero

An AI due-diligence questionnaire lands, and four people spend a fortnight reconstructing answers from Slack, tickets and memory.

1 list

Everything downstream needs the same list

Assessments, the Statement of Applicability, the risk register, the trust centre and the audit pack are all views of one register — or they disagree.

AI inventory

The list has to exist before anything else can be true

Register every AI system — bought, integrated or built — with its vendor, its owner, its lifecycle stage and what it is permitted to be given. Import a spreadsheet on day one; the plan is shown before a single row is written, and invalid rows are rejected rather than quietly coerced.

Origin, owner, stage and vendor on every row — the fields the rest of the system reads.
Coverage per system, computed live against ISO 42001 — never a stored number that drifts.
Export the register as CSV, XLSX or PDF with the columns you picked, ready to attach to an answer.

Use cases

A system and a use case are different objects

Risk does not live at the tool. One assistant that both drafts meeting notes and ranks job applicants is two entirely different conversations — and only one of them is high risk. Forseti models the purpose separately from the system, and lets a single pairing carry its own tier where it genuinely differs.

Many-to-many by design — a purpose served by three tools counts for all three.
Baseline plus override — the purpose sets the tier; a specific pairing may raise it.
Human oversight recorded per use case, and published as the weakest arrangement, never the flattering one.

Intake & triage

Origin decides the questions

Procured SaaS, an API integration and a model built in house raise different questions — same workflow, different question set. A request arrives, the triage rules recommend the review type from the answers themselves, and a reviewer either agrees or departs from it on the record.

The recommendation is computed server-side, so nobody can submit a request that has already agreed with itself.
A departure is measured and shown — an override needs a justification, not a shrug.
An endorsed request becomes a registered system — the queue is where the inventory grows from.

Statement of Applicability

Applicability is a join, not a property of a control

Your Statement of Applicability is that join — per framework, per control, per system. Controls about training data and model design do not apply to an organisation that buys its AI, so the SoA arrives with a defensible consumption-only posture already argued, rather than eighty rows to copy-paste a rationale into.

An exclusion without a written rationale is refused by the server, not just discouraged by the form.
Evidence is cited against the control it proves — and coverage falls the day that document expires.
Claim controls against systems, so "who does this apply to" has an answer with names in it.

How the AI is used

AI proposes, humans dispose — and everything is audited

A suggestion changes nothing until a person accepts it. The trail records both the person and the suggestion they accepted, and the model is never asked to score its own confidence or invent a coverage figure. Code owns every number on the screen.

Drafts the prose you would have written

Exclusion rationales, risk descriptions, impact assessment narrative — written from the records already on file, never from thin air.

Answers questionnaires from the register

Every answer is grounded in a record and cites it. An answer can never claim more than the inventory supports.

Leaves a trail an auditor can follow

Append-only activity, per-field. Who suggested, who accepted, when, and what the record said before.
